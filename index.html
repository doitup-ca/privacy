Privacy Policy

Covering the Android applications published by Do It Up, including Hearth (listed as Hearth Launcher).

LAST UPDATED  SEPTEMBER 26, 2026 ONTARIO, CANADA
THE SHORT VERSION

These apps have no accounts, and we run no servers for them. What they save stays on your device. They connect to the services you set up — your Home Assistant server and your live TV provider — and to services your device already provides, such as speech recognition for voice search.

The one thing we do hold is not the app at all. If you write to us — to ask about testing Hearth before it is released, or about anything else — then we have what you sent. Section 06 says what happens to it, and it is short.

01

We do not operate a server

Do It Up runs no backend for these apps. There is no sign-up, no account, no profile, and no login. Nothing you enter is sent to us, and we have no ability to see, retrieve, or delete your data — because we never receive it in the first place.

That is about the app. A message you send us is different: it reaches a mailbox we read. See if you write to us.

Entitlement, where it applies, is handled entirely by Google Play under Google’s own terms. We do not receive your payment details.

02

What is stored on your device

Hearth stores the following locally, on the device only:

WhatWhyHow
Home Assistant address and access token To connect to your home automation system Encrypted — Android Keystore
Live TV address, and a username and password if your provider uses one To load your channels and guide Encrypted — Android Keystore
Preferences — favourites, channel groups, watch history, theme, layout, hidden rooms and cards To keep the app set up the way you left it Standard app storage
Program guide cache So the guide loads without re-downloading Local database
Channel logo images So logos load without re-downloading App cache, up to 16 MB
Your Home Assistant dashboard’s web files and settings, if you open the dashboard in Hearth So the dashboard opens quickly. These are Home Assistant’s own files, kept by Android’s web view App storage

Your logins are encrypted while they are stored on the device. How they travel to the service they belong to is covered in section 03. Automatic cloud backup is turned off, so they are not copied to Google’s backup service.

Uninstalling the app deletes everything above, and we hold no copy. The access token Hearth used stays valid in your Home Assistant until you remove it there, in your Home Assistant user profile.

03

Who your device talks to

The app connects only to services you have chosen or that your device provides. None of them are run by us.

Your Home Assistant server

The address is one you supply, usually on your own local network. Your access token is sent to it to authenticate, exactly as any Home Assistant client does. Your home data does not pass through us.

Your live TV provider

The addresses, and any credentials, are ones you supply. Your provider necessarily sees your requests — what you play and when — and handles that under their privacy policy, not this one.

Channel logos load from wherever your provider’s channel list points, and some of those sites have no encryption. Anyone on your network could see which logos load, and so which channels you are browsing.

PROVIDERS WITHOUT ENCRYPTION

The app tries an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to your provider first. If your provider doesn’t offer one, the app asks before connecting and explains what it means: your provider username and password, and what you watch, can be seen by anyone on your network and by your internet provider. That permission covers that one provider; a different provider asks again. A playlist is stricter: it names hundreds of hosts you have never seen, so the app does not ask — unencrypted playlists, guides and channels are refused.

On your own home network, the app also connects without encryption where that is how a service is set up — for example, a Home Assistant server at a local address. Other unencrypted connections that the app makes itself are refused. If you choose to show your Home Assistant dashboard in the app, it is a web page and loads whatever that dashboard loads, including anything a custom card fetches from other sites.

Voice search

Voice search uses your device’s speech recognition service — on many devices, Google’s — which handles the audio under its own privacy policy and returns the words to the app. The app does not save or send any audio. The microphone is on only while voice search is listening, and turns off when it returns a result or you close it.

04

What we do not do

No analytics. No analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs are built in.
No crash reports from the app. Google Play may collect crash and performance data about apps on your device, under Google’s own terms.
No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
No tracking across apps, sites, or devices.
No selling or sharing of personal information — including anything you send us by email. It is not sold, rented, traded, or passed to anyone for their own use.

Accessibility service (optional) Lets Hearth come back to the front when another launcher takes the screen, and lets Sleep in the dock turn the screen off. The accessibility service cannot read your screen. It learns which app has come to the front, and sees remote and keyboard button presses so it can return to Hearth; it passes every press straight through and records none of them. It learns nothing about what is on screen.

Location permission is not requested.

06

If you write to us

Everything above is about the app, and about a device we never hear from. This section is the other case: you sending us a message, which is the only way we come to hold anything about you at all.

We do not ask for it, and there is no form to fill in. What we have is what you chose to put in an email.

WhatWhyHow long
Your email address To reply — and, if you asked to test Hearth, to enrol you in the test While the test runs, or while the conversation is useful
Whatever you tell us — which TV box you have, whether you run Home Assistant, what went wrong To answer you, and to know what the app has to cope with Kept with the message

Mail arrives in a mailbox we hold with an email provider, and is read by us. It is not added to a mailing list, a newsletter or any analytics.

TESTING BEFORE RELEASE

Hearth is in closed testing, and access is granted by Google account rather than by download. If you ask to take part, the address you send us is entered into the tester list in Google Play Console — that is the mechanism Google provides, and there is no way to give you the app without it. Google handles that list under its own terms, as the operator of the store.

That is the whole of it. The address is used to grant access and to talk to you about the app, and for nothing else.

Ask at any time and we will take you off the list. Doing so ends your access to the test build — that is what being on the list is — and the address goes with it. When the closed test ends, the list is deleted.

Our website has no forms, no accounts and no analytics.

07

These apps are not directed at children, and the apps collect no personal information from users of any age. We do not knowingly seek information from a child by any other route either.
